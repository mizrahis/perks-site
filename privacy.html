Perks privacy notice

Last updated 19 October 2026

Perks helps you keep track of the benefits you already pay for. It is free and has no adverts. Your account, and what you add to it, are stored in the EU. Perks uses no tracking and no third-party analytics or advertising code; it records a handful of its own product events, described below. To find benefits in what you share, Perks sends it to two AI services, but only after you give permission, and you can withdraw that permission at any time. Reminders are sent from Perks’ servers through Apple once your phone is registered, and, if you switch on nearby nudges, your phone searches Apple Maps around where it is, which goes to Apple and not to Perks. You can delete your account, and everything saved with it, from inside the app.

Who we are

Perks is made by Mizrahi Ltd (“we”) and is published on the App Store by Rip Club Ltd on our behalf. Mizrahi Ltd decides how the information below is used, so we are its controller under UK data protection law. You can reach us at vedatm@gmail.com.

What Perks stores about you

  • Your account. The email address and password you sign up with. Your password is kept in a protected (hashed) form by the sign-in service; we never see it. A guest account (see below) has neither.
  • What you add. The memberships you hold and, if you give it, the date a membership renews, the subscriptions you pay for and what you pay for them, the uses you log against a benefit, and the choices you make, such as which extra you picked, that you have enrolled in an offer, or what you decided about a subscription that a membership already includes. Perks only records these; it never enrols you, cancels anything or contacts a provider for you.
  • Reports and notes. If you tap “This didn’t work” on a benefit, Perks keeps the reason you chose and the note you may add (up to 280 characters), with your account. The note is text you write yourself, so please leave personal details out of it. We read these reports to correct the catalogue, and for nothing else.
  • Product events. Perks records a few events of its own (for example that a benefit was shown, opened or marked used, or that you created an account), so that we can see which parts of the app are useful. Each event carries its name, the time, and the identifier of your account (a random code, not your email address). Where it applies it also carries the identifiers of the benefit, membership or programme it was about, a few short words such as the kind of row it came from, and, for one event, how many memberships you confirmed. It never carries names, email addresses, prices, dates or free text. Your account can add events but cannot read them back; only we can read them.

We use the first two only to run the app for you: to show your benefits, what is still unused, and what you may be paying for twice. Our reason is that we need them to provide the app you asked for. We use the reports and the events to improve Perks, which is our legitimate interest in doing so. We do not sell any of it or use it for anything else.

Where it is stored

Your account and everything you add are stored with Supabase, which provides Perks’ database, sign-in, file storage and server functions, in its EU region (eu-west-1). Supabase acts on our behalf as a data processor. Every table is protected by row-level security, so your account can read and change only its own rows.

When you first open Perks it creates a guest account for you, with no email address, so you can see your first result before deciding whether to keep it. Everything you add is saved to that guest account on our servers from the start, not only on your phone; only your phone can open it. Choosing “Keep your results” attaches your email and password to that same account, so nothing is copied or lost.

If you sign out of a guest account, the app deletes it, and everything in it, after telling you so. It does the same if you sign in to an account you already have: the app tells you that what you added as a guest will be deleted and, once you agree, signs you in and then deletes the guest account (if the sign-in does not work, for example because the password is wrong, nothing is deleted; if you have added nothing, it simply deletes the empty guest account). If the guest account cannot be deleted at that moment, for example because you are offline, it is left to be removed automatically, as below. A guest account that nobody uses again (for example because the phone is lost, or Perks is deleted from it) is removed automatically once it has gone unused for 90 days; using Perks starts the 90 days again. If you never create an account and never sign out, the guest data stays for as long as you use it, until you delete it from You, Delete account.

Perks also keeps logs of its server functions, to keep the service working and within budget. A log line holds the time, your account’s identifier, the kind of request, counts, statuses and durations. It never holds what you shared, an email, a token or a key. These logs are kept by Supabase for its own log retention period, which we do not set.

AI services and your permission

Perks finds benefits by sending what you share to two AI services: Anthropic (its Claude model reads the text, page or image and extracts the memberships, subscriptions, benefits and offers it describes) and TypeSafe (its Jev model decides whether a piece of text is about a membership, subscription, benefit or offer). They are separate companies from Perks, and they may process what they are sent outside the UK and the EU.

  • Permission first. The first time you share something, or paste it into Perks, the app asks “Let Perks read what you share?” and sends nothing until you choose Allow and import. Choosing Not now sends nothing. The same permission covers Gmail, if you connect it. It is asked again if the AI services or the purpose change.
  • What is sent. The text, link or image you share and, if you connect Gmail, the emails that look like membership mail. When you share a page from Safari, what is sent is the page’s address and the text that is visible on it, up to 100 KB, because many pages that matter cannot be fetched without signing in; nothing else on your phone is read.
  • Why. To find benefits, offers and renewals for you, and for nothing else.
  • How long. Images are deleted once read. Perks keeps the structured result and, with each finding, a short excerpt of up to 300 characters so that you can see why it was found, never the whole text or page. The AI services process the content to answer and do not use it to train their models under their API terms.
  • Withdrawing. You can withdraw permission at any time in You, under AI processing. That stops new processing. Content already sent cannot be recalled.

What Perks finds waits in your Inbox, and nothing becomes a membership, a subscription or a use until you confirm it, in the Inbox or by choosing Add in the share sheet. An offer that a provider’s own page or email states plainly may be put with your offers straight away, and you can dismiss it there.

When you share a screenshot or a photo instead, your phone first shrinks it to at most 1,600 pixels on its longest side and leaves out everything stored with it, such as where it was taken. The shrunk image is uploaded to a private folder in our storage that only your account and our import service can open, and the import service has Anthropic’s Claude read it; the text Claude reads from it is checked by TypeSafe’s Jev, as above. The image is deleted as soon as it has been read, whether or not anything was found in it. If sending fails, or you close the share sheet before it has finished, your phone tries to delete the image it uploaded. If that does not work either (for example, because the connection is lost or the phone loses power), our server removes any image left in that folder, within a day. The same applies when you delete your account and when a guest account is removed.

What stays on your phone

  • Your answers to the two questions Perks asks before it looks at your phone (“Found on your phone” and “Scan a card”), and your choice of light or dark appearance.
  • A saved copy of what Home last showed, so that Perks can open without a connection.
  • A note that an account has been used on this phone, so that after you sign out Perks opens on the sign-in screen rather than starting a new guest account.
  • Shares waiting to be sent. If you share a page, a link or some text to Perks while your phone is offline, or our import service cannot be reached, Perks keeps what you shared (the address and the text of the page) in a private file that only the app and its share extension can open, until the app next opens and sends it; it is then deleted. Anything still waiting is deleted unread when you sign out or delete your account, and the file is left out of your phone’s backups. If you choose Review later in the share sheet, your phone is asked to show a notice that says how many things Perks found, and nothing about them; like a reminder, it is made by your phone itself.
  • Calendar. If you choose Add dates to Calendar on You, Perks asks your phone for access to your calendar and makes a calendar called Perks on it. Your phone calls this full access, because it offers no narrower way to take back the events Perks added earlier; Perks uses it for that one calendar only. It puts the dates it would remind you about there as all-day events: the last day of a credit, when a benefit ends and when a membership renews. Each event carries the name of the benefit or membership and one short line, never an amount, and adding the dates again replaces them. Perks reads and changes events in that calendar alone and never looks at an event in any other, and nothing is sent to our servers. Your phone may still sync the Perks calendar to the calendar account it sits in (for example iCloud), as it does any calendar. You can delete the calendar in Calendar, or switch the access off in Settings, at any time.
  • Reminders on the phone. Perks can remind you before a credit resets, an offer ends or a membership renews. It asks your phone for permission only after you save your first action, or when you choose Review later in the share sheet, never when the app opens. Some reminders are scheduled by your phone itself (for example a benefit that ends, or a cancellation notice), and the notice that Review later shows is made by your phone and says how many things Perks found, and nothing about them. The rest are sent from our servers, as described under Reminders from our servers below. Either way, a reminder names the benefit or membership it is about and, for a credit, how much of it is unused (for example “£150 unused on The Platinum Card”), so like any notification it can appear on your lock screen. If you tap a reminder, Perks records that you did, as one of the product events above, with the identifier of what it was about and the word “notification”, and none of the reminder’s text.

Signing out, or deleting your account, clears the saved copy and your answers, removes the reminders that are waiting on your phone and any already delivered to Notification Centre, takes this phone off your account’s list of devices so that our servers stop sending to it, removes the widget’s file, and takes the dates Perks added out of the Perks calendar. Your appearance choice, your reminder choices (the hour and quiet hours) and the note that an account has been used here stay.

Reminders from our servers

Once your phone allows notifications and registers with us, reminders for credits, renewals and saved offers are sent from Perks’ servers through Apple’s push notification service (APNs), which delivers them to your phone. To do this, our servers keep, for each phone you use: Apple’s push token for it, whether it is a test or live build, the app and iOS versions, your phone’s language and time zone, the hour you chose for reminders and your quiet hours. They also keep each reminder they make: its title and text (the name of the benefit or membership and, for a credit, the unused amount), and where a tap goes. Apple receives the token and the reminder in order to deliver it. We use the token only to send you reminders; it is not used to track you or sent to anyone else.

Until your phone has registered, your phone makes these reminders itself, so none is missed and none is told twice. Signing out removes the phone from your account’s list; if your phone is offline at that moment, the entry is removed when Apple tells us the token is no longer valid. Deleting your account deletes the devices and reminders.

Nearby nudges (optional)

If you switch on Nearby nudges on You, Perks asks your phone for location access, and your phone tells Perks when its position has changed a good deal (cell towers and wifi; never continuous GPS, and it works with Perks closed). For each saved offer or benefit you hold that names a merchant, your phone searches Apple Maps for a shop of that name within about 500 metres of where it is. That search, with the merchant’s name and the area around your position, goes to Apple, under Apple’s privacy policy. It does not go to Perks: your position is used on your phone to decide whether to say “Gail’s, 200 m away” and is then dropped, and Perks does not store or collect it. You can switch nudges off on You, or location access off in Settings, at any time.

The widget

If you add the Perks widget, the app writes a small file that the widget reads on your phone: the title and date of your next deadline, up to three more, what you still have to use, and what you have kept this year. The widget makes no network request. Because a widget can sit on your lock screen, anyone who can see your phone’s lock screen can see those deadlines and amounts, as with a notification; you can change this in your phone’s lock-screen settings or by removing the widget. The file is removed when you sign out or delete your account.

Logos

The logos of programmes and services in Perks are fetched by our servers from each brand’s own site and kept in a public folder in our storage. The app loads them by an address that names a programme, not you. They hold no personal information.

Your camera and your apps

Both are optional, and Perks asks first.

  • Scan a card. If you allow the camera, Perks reads the words printed on a card, on your phone, to find its programme. Card numbers are ignored and never kept. Nothing the camera sees is saved or leaves your phone, and a card is only added when you confirm.
  • Found on your phone. If you say yes, Perks checks whether certain apps (at present Revolut’s) are installed, so that it can suggest memberships. Nothing is read from those apps, and the answer stays on your phone.

Gmail (optional)

If you choose Connect Gmail on You (you need an account for this, not a guest session), Perks asks Google for read-only access to your Gmail (the gmail.readonly permission) so that it can find the memberships, subscriptions and offers in your email. Before it connects, the app asks the AI permission described above, because reading your mail means sending parts of it to the AI services. That permission lets Perks read your mail; it does not let it send, delete or change anything, and Perks asks for nothing else of your Google account.

  • What Perks reads. About every two hours, and once, in the background, for the last 12 months, Perks looks at who each message is from, its subject and date, and the short preview Gmail gives of it. A message that has nothing to do with the memberships, subscriptions and offers Perks knows about is set aside there, and its text is never read. For the rest, our servers read the message and send its text (the sender and subject, and up to 12,000 characters) to TypeSafe’s Jev, to judge whether it is about a membership, subscription, benefit or offer, and then, if it is, to Anthropic’s Claude, to pick out what it says. Neither provider uses it to train its models. Spam, Trash, Drafts and Sent are never looked at.
  • What Perks keeps. The ids of the messages it has looked at and the day each arrived, with what it decided (set aside, not about benefits, read) and the scores behind that, which hold none of the message’s words. For what it finds, an entry in your Inbox: a title and a short summary, the sender’s domain, the message’s subject, a link that opens the email in Gmail, and an excerpt of at most 300 characters quoted from it. Perks never keeps an email, its attachments or its full text. An offer that a provider’s own email states plainly may be put with your offers straight away, and you can dismiss it. A use of a credit that a receipt in your email plainly shows, when the credit is yours and enough is left of it, may be logged for you, marked “from a receipt”, and you can undo it. Nothing becomes a membership or a subscription until you confirm it.
  • The key to your Gmail. Google gives Perks a token that lets it keep reading. It is kept encrypted in our database’s vault, used only by our servers and never sent to your phone. While Perks is still in Google’s testing stage, Google ends it after 7 days: Perks then says Needs reconnect on You and you connect again.
  • Taking it back. Disconnect on You takes the token back from Google, so that Perks can no longer read your mail, deletes it from our vault, and deletes the list of messages. If Google cannot be reached at that moment, Perks still deletes its own copy and tells you that Google may still list Perks, which you can remove yourself (below). What Perks already found stays in your Inbox and offers until you dismiss it or delete your account. Deleting your account disconnects Gmail first and deletes all of it. You can also withdraw Perks’ access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Perks’ use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: it is used only to show you what is in your own email, and people at Perks do not read it unless you ask them to, to look into abuse, or because the law requires it.

What we do not do

Perks is free and shows no adverts. It uses no tracking code, no analytics service and no advertising, and it does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites. The product events described above stay in our own database. We do not sell your information. It goes only to: Supabase, which stores it for us; Anthropic and TypeSafe, for what you ask Perks to read and have given permission for; Google, for the Gmail access you choose to give; Apple, to deliver reminders (the push token and the reminder) and, if you switch on nearby nudges, to answer the map search; and the email service described next.

Emails

When you create an account or ask for a password reset, Perks asks Supabase to send you a message, which goes out through the email service connected to our project. Perks does not send marketing emails.

How long we keep it

For as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, your account and everything saved with it are removed from our database straight away, and any uploaded image still in our storage is removed by our server within a day. Routine backups kept by our hosting provider may hold a copy for a short while afterwards; they are not used for anything else. A guest account is deleted when its guest signs out of it or signs in to an account they already have, and a guest account that has gone unused for 90 days is removed automatically. What Apple and the AI services hold once they have received something is covered by their own terms and privacy policies.

Deleting your account

In the app, open You and choose Delete account. Once you confirm, your email address and password, your memberships and subscriptions, the uses and choices you have recorded, the reports and notes you have sent, the product events recorded under your account, the devices and reminders registered for pushes and, if you connected Gmail, that connection (taken back from Google first, and its token deleted) are deleted, uploaded images are removed by our server within a day, and what Perks saved on your phone is cleared. This cannot be undone. A guest can do the same, or sign out (or sign in to an account they already have), which deletes the guest account and everything in it. If you cannot use the app, email us and we will delete the account for you.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, restrict or object to how it is used, and have it given to you in a portable form. Deleting your account in the app does the most common of these at once; for the rest, email us. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this notice

If this notice changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes with it. The Privacy link in the app always opens the current version.
