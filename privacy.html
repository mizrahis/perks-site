Perks privacy notice

Last updated 6 October 2026

Perks helps you keep track of the benefits you already pay for. Your account, and what you add to it, are stored in the EU. Perks uses no third-party analytics, no advertising and no tracking; it records a handful of its own product events, described below. Reminders are made on your phone and never leave it. You can delete your account, and everything saved with it, from inside the app.

Who we are

Perks is published by Rip Club Ltd (“we”). We decide how the information below is used, so we are its controller under UK data protection law. You can reach us at vmizrahi@decantgroup.com.

What Perks stores about you

  • Your account. The email address and password you sign up with. Your password is kept in a protected (hashed) form by the sign-in service; we never see it. A guest account (see below) has neither.
  • What you add. The memberships you hold and, if you give it, the date a membership renews, the subscriptions you pay for and what you pay for them, the uses you log against a benefit, and the choices you make, such as which extra you picked, that you have enrolled in an offer, or what you decided about a subscription that a membership already includes. Perks only records these; it never enrols you, cancels anything or contacts a provider for you.
  • Reports and notes. If you tap “This didn’t work” on a benefit, Perks keeps the reason you chose and the note you may add (up to 280 characters), with your account. The note is text you write yourself, so please leave personal details out of it. We read these reports to correct the catalogue, and for nothing else.
  • Product events. Perks records a few events of its own (for example that a benefit was shown, opened or marked used, or that you created an account), so that we can see which parts of the app are useful. Each event carries its name, the time, and the identifier of your account (a random code, not your email address). Where it applies it also carries the identifiers of the benefit, membership or programme it was about, a few short words such as the kind of row it came from, and, for one event, how many memberships you confirmed. It never carries names, email addresses, prices, dates or free text. Your account can add events but cannot read them back; only we can read them.

We use the first two only to run the app for you: to show your benefits, what is still unused, and what you may be paying for twice. Our reason is that we need them to provide the app you asked for. We use the reports and the events to improve Perks, which is our legitimate interest in doing so. We do not sell any of it or use it for anything else.

Where it is stored

When you paste text or a link into Perks, or share a web page, a link or some text to it, that content is sent to our import service, which uses AI models (Anthropic's Claude and TypeSafe's Jev, each via its API) to pick out the memberships, subscriptions, benefits and offers it describes. When you share a page from Safari, what is sent is the page's address and the text that is visible on it, up to 100 KB, because many of the pages that matter cannot be fetched without signing in; nothing else on your phone is read. Perks keeps only the structured result and a short excerpt of up to 300 characters as evidence, never the whole text or page. What Perks finds waits in your Inbox, and nothing becomes a membership, a subscription or a use until you confirm it, in the Inbox or by choosing Add in the share sheet. An offer that a provider’s own page or email states plainly may be put with your offers straight away, and you can dismiss it there. The AI providers process the text to answer the request and do not use it to train their models.

When you share a screenshot or a photo instead, your phone first shrinks it to at most 1,600 pixels on its longest side and leaves out everything stored with it, such as where it was taken. The shrunk image is uploaded to a private folder in our storage that only your account and our import service can open, and the import service has Anthropic's Claude read it; the text Claude reads from it is checked by TypeSafe's Jev, as above. The image is deleted as soon as it has been read, whether or not anything was found in it, and Perks keeps only the structured result and a short excerpt, as above. If sending fails, or you close the share sheet before it has finished, your phone tries to delete the image it uploaded. If that does not work either (for example, because the connection is lost or the phone loses power), the image can stay in that private folder until you delete your account from You, Delete account, which removes it. A guest account that is deleted another way (by signing in to an account you already have, or automatically after 90 days unused) does not yet take such an image with it.

When you first open Perks it creates a guest account for you, with no email address, so you can see your first result before deciding whether to keep it. Everything you add is saved to that guest account on our servers from the start, not only on your phone; only your phone can open it. Choosing “Keep your results” attaches your email and password to that same account, so nothing is copied or lost.

If you sign out of a guest account, the app deletes it, and everything in it, after telling you so. It does the same if you sign in to an account you already have: the app tells you that what you added as a guest will be deleted and, once you agree, signs you in and then deletes the guest account (if the sign-in does not work, for example because the password is wrong, nothing is deleted; if you have added nothing, it simply deletes the empty guest account). If the guest account cannot be deleted at that moment, for example because you are offline, it is left to be removed automatically, as below. A guest account that nobody uses again (for example because the phone is lost, or Perks is deleted from it) is removed automatically once it has gone unused for 90 days; using Perks starts the 90 days again. If you never create an account and never sign out, the guest data stays for as long as you use it, until you delete it from You, Delete account.

This information is stored with Supabase, which provides Perks’ database and sign-in, in its EU region (eu-west-1). Supabase acts on our behalf as a data processor. Every table is protected by row-level security, so your account can read and change only its own rows.

What stays on your phone

  • Your answers to the two questions Perks asks before it looks at your phone (“Found on your phone” and “Scan a card”), and your choice of light or dark appearance.
  • A saved copy of what Home last showed, so that Perks can open without a connection.
  • A note that an account has been used on this phone, so that after you sign out Perks opens on the sign-in screen rather than starting a new guest account.
  • Shares waiting to be sent. If you share a page, a link or some text to Perks while your phone is offline, or our import service cannot be reached, Perks keeps what you shared (the address and the text of the page) in a private file that only the app and its share extension can open, until the app next opens and sends it; it is then deleted. Anything still waiting is deleted unread when you sign out or delete your account, and the file is left out of your phone’s backups. If you choose Review later in the share sheet, your phone is asked to show a notice that says how many things Perks found, and nothing about them; like a reminder, it is made by your phone itself.
  • Calendar. If you choose Add dates to Calendar on You, Perks asks your phone for access to your calendar and makes a calendar called Perks on it. Your phone calls this full access, because it offers no narrower way to take back the events Perks added earlier; Perks uses it for that one calendar only. It puts the dates it would remind you about there as all-day events: the last day of a credit, when a benefit ends and when a membership renews. Each event carries the name of the benefit or membership and one short line, never an amount, and adding the dates again replaces them. Perks reads and changes events in that calendar alone and never looks at an event in any other, and nothing is sent to our servers. Your phone may still sync the Perks calendar to the calendar account it sits in (for example iCloud), as it does any calendar. You can delete the calendar in Calendar, or switch the access off in Settings, at any time.
  • Reminders. Perks can remind you before a credit resets, an offer ends or a membership renews. It asks your phone for permission only after you save your first action, or when you choose Review later in the share sheet, never when the app opens. Reminders are scheduled by your phone itself: they are never sent from, or through, our servers. A reminder names the benefit or membership it is about and, for a credit, how much of it is unused (for example “£150 unused on The Platinum Card”), so like any notification it can appear on your lock screen. Whether reminders are on, the hour they arrive and your quiet hours are kept on your phone, not on our servers. If you tap a reminder, Perks records that you did, as one of the product events above, with the identifier of what it was about and the word “notification”, and none of the reminder’s text.

Signing out, or deleting your account, clears the saved copy and your answers, removes the reminders that are waiting on your phone and any already delivered to Notification Centre, and takes the dates Perks added out of the Perks calendar. Your appearance choice, your reminder choices and the note that an account has been used here stay.

Your camera and your apps

Both are optional, and Perks asks first.

  • Scan a card. If you allow the camera, Perks reads the words printed on a card, on your phone, to find its programme. Card numbers are ignored and never kept. Nothing the camera sees is saved or leaves your phone, and a card is only added when you confirm.
  • Found on your phone. If you say yes, Perks checks whether certain apps (at present Revolut’s) are installed, so that it can suggest memberships. Nothing is read from those apps, and the answer stays on your phone.

What we do not do

Perks uses no analytics service and no advertising, and it does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites. The product events described above stay in our own database. We share your information with no one except Supabase, which stores it for us, and the email service described next.

Emails

When you create an account or ask for a password reset, Perks asks Supabase to send you a message, which goes out through the email service connected to our project. Perks does not send marketing emails.

How long we keep it

For as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, your account and everything saved with it are removed from our database straight away. Routine backups kept by our hosting provider may hold a copy for a short while afterwards; they are not used for anything else. A guest account is deleted when its guest signs out of it or signs in to an account they already have, and a guest account that has gone unused for 90 days is removed automatically.

Deleting your account

In the app, open You and choose Delete account. Once you confirm, your email address and password, your memberships and subscriptions, the uses and choices you have recorded, the reports and notes you have sent, and the product events recorded under your account are deleted, and what Perks saved on your phone is cleared. This cannot be undone. A guest can do the same, or sign out (or sign in to an account they already have), which deletes the guest account and everything in it. If you cannot use the app, email us and we will delete the account for you.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, restrict or object to how it is used, and have it given to you in a portable form. Deleting your account in the app does the most common of these at once; for the rest, email us. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this notice

If this notice changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes with it. The Privacy link in the app always opens the current version.
